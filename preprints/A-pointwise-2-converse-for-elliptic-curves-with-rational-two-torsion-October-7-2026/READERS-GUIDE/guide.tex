\section{The theorem, the state of the art, and why $p=2$ is hard}
\label{sec:thm}

\subsection{Statement}
For $A/\Q$ put $r(A)=\rank A(\Q)$, $a(A)=\ord_{s=1}L(A,s)$ and
\[
 s_2(A)=\corank_{\Z_2}\Sel_{2^\infty}(A/\Q),
\]
with the local Kummer image at every place. By \lbl{pr:ranks}
(\src{preliminaries}{13}), $s_2(A)=r(A)+\corank_{\Z_2}\Sha(A/\Q)[2^\infty]$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Theorem 1.1} (\lbl{thm:main}, \src{introduction}{22}).
\emph{Let $E/\Q$ be an elliptic curve with $E(\Q)[2]\ne0$ and
$s_2(E)\in\{0,1\}$. Then}
\[
 \ord_{s=1}L(E,s)=\rank_\Z E(\Q)=s_2(E),\qquad \Sha(E/\Q)\text{ is finite.}
\]

\begin{inwords}
A $2^\infty$-Selmer corank of $0$ or $1$ forces the analytic rank to equal
that corank. Gross--Zagier--Kolyvagin then gives the rank and finiteness of
$\Sha$. The hypothesis is the full Selmer \emph{corank}; it is never replaced
by a finite $2$-Selmer dimension or by the Mordell--Weil rank.
\end{inwords}

The theorem covers one rational $2$-torsion line or full rational
$2$-torsion, CM and non-CM curves, every isogeny configuration, and every
reduction type at $2$ and at the bad primes (\src{introduction}{32--37}).
It does not prove the BSD leading-term formula. The broader companion
\cite{OAIGoldfeld} proves a $2$-converse without the $2$-torsion hypothesis,
but is not an input here.

\subsection{Why $p=2$ with rational $2$-torsion is hard}
\label{sec:hard}
A Kolyvagin- or Kato-style converse at an odd prime $p$ uses, implicitly or
explicitly, the following steps. Each one fails or loses control at $p=2$
when $E[2]$ has a rational point.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{$\tau$-eigenspace splitting.} Complex conjugation $\tau$ splits
 cohomology into $\pm$ eigenspaces via the idempotents $(1\pm\tau)/2$.
 At $p=2$ these are not integral.
\item \emph{Vanishing of $H^1(\Gal(K(E[2^n])/K),E[2^n])$.} For $p$ odd and
 large image this gives injectivity of restriction to $K(E[p^n])$ and
 clean descent of Kolyvagin derivative classes. At $2$ it fails in general.
\item \emph{Residual irreducibility.} $E[2]$ is reducible by hypothesis.
 Classically, irreducibility controls Selmer groups of $T/p^n$, gives
 Kolyvagin-system rank bounds, and makes $\Lambda$-adic control work.
\item \emph{Real places and Tate cohomology.} Over $\Q$ the real place
 contributes $2$-torsion Tate cohomology. Nekov\'a\v{r}'s duality framework
 assumes his condition (P), ``if $p=2$ then the base field has no real
 prime'', and treats its absence separately
 \cite[\S\S5.7, 6.9]{Nekovar2006}.
\item \emph{Kato's integral divisibility.} Kato's integral statement
 \cite[12.5(4)]{Kato2004} (and 13.4(3)) needs $p\ne2$, according to our
 audit's reading of Kato. Only the rational divisibility \cite[12.5(3)]{Kato2004}
 is available at $2$.
\item \emph{Small numerical steps.} Examples are the factor
 $\ell(\ell+1)/2$ in Kolyvagin's derivative, isotropy of local planes
 deduced from skew-symmetry, and inertia projectors $(1+g)/2$. Each costs
 a factor $2$ \emph{per prime}, which is fatal when the number of primes is
 unbounded.
\end{enumerate}
Section~\ref{sec:replace} lists what replaces each step.

\section{The missing-vertex idea}
\label{sec:mv}

Index quadratic twists by a binary cube. Fix a base $h_0$ (the curve being
tested is $E^{(h_0)}$; in the end $h_0=1$). Choose primes $q\in Q$ and
linear forms $\lambda_q:\F_2^b\to\F_2$, and put
\[
 h_x=h_0\prod_{q\in Q}(q^*)^{\lambda_q(x)},\qquad x\in\F_2^b,\qquad
 q^*=\bigl(\tfrac{-1}{q}\bigr)q .
\]
Address $0$ is the unknown curve. The arithmetic constructs one element of a
group ring $\Z_2[G]$, $G=(\Z/2)^b$ (with power-series coefficients in
practice). Up to a uniformly bounded error, the valuation of its character
value at $x$ is the normalized valuation of the $L$-value, or of the Heegner
index, of $E^{(h_x)}$. The core is the following abstract
statement, which is our simplified composite of Lemma~\lbl{cy:binary}
(\src{cyclotomic}{121}) and the proof of Theorem~\lbl{cy:missing-vertex}
(\src{cyclotomic}{763}).

\begin{lemma}[Abstract missing vertex]\label{lem:A}
Let $G=(\Z/2)^b$ with characters indexed by $x\in\F_2^b$, and let
$u\in\Q_2[G]$ have character values $u_x$. Suppose
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{(uniform integrality)} $2^{C}u\in\Z_2[G]$;
\item \emph{(bounded valuations off the base)} $u_x\ne0$ and $v_2(u_x)\le B$
 for every $x\ne0$.
\end{enumerate}
If $M>B+C$ and $b>2^M-1$, then $u_0\ne0$ and $v_2(u_0)\le B$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Write $z=2^Cu\in\Z_2[G]$. Expanding each sign character as $1-2x_i$ gives
$z(x)=\sum_I 2^{|I|}a_I\prod_{i\in I}x_i$ with $a_I\in\Z_2$. The $j$-th
binary digit of $z(x)$ is a Boolean polynomial in $x$ of degree at most
$2^j$. So ``$z(x)\equiv z(0)\bmod 2^M$'' is a system of Boolean equations of
total degree at most $2^M-1<b$. By the characteristic-$2$
Chevalley--Warning count, the number of common zeros is even; since $x=0$ is
one, there is an $x\ne0$ with $z_x\equiv z_0\pmod{2^M}$. As
$v_2(z_x)\le B+C<M$, this forces $v_2(z_0)=v_2(z_x)$.
\end{proof}

With $r$ simultaneous tests the threshold is $b>r(2^M-1)$
(\lbl{cy:binary}). The ring-class version, \lbl{rd:bits} and
\lbl{rd:binary-transfer} (\src{ring-determinants}{1620--1699}), tests
$L(M)+2$ Laurent coefficients at once.

\paragraph{Why $b$ must be unbounded, and why $B$ must not depend on $b$.}
Fourier inversion shows which functions $x\mapsto u_x$ come from
$\Z_2[G]$. A simple example (ours, checked by hand) is
\[
 u=2^b\cdot1-\sum_{g\in G}g,\qquad u_0=0,\qquad u_x=2^b\ (x\ne0).
\]
Here $u$ is integral, every nonzero vertex has valuation exactly $b$, and
the base vanishes. So a bound of the form $B\ge b$ proves nothing, and for a
fixed $B$ the cube must have dimension $b>B$; the lemma asks for
$b>2^{B+C+1}-1$.

\begin{inwords}
The arithmetic must deliver (1) one integral family whose denominator $C$ does
not grow with $b$, $\#Q$ or the prime count of $h_0$, and (2) a valuation
bound $B$ at the nonzero vertices that is equally uniform. Only then may $b$
be taken large. Almost every technical device in the paper serves one of
these two uniformities at the prime~$2$.
\end{inwords}

\paragraph{The two instances.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Cyclotomic, rank $0$} (\lbl{cy:missing-vertex},
 \src{cyclotomic}{45}). Here $u\in\Lambda[G]$ with $2^{C_{\rm int}}u\in
 \Lambda[G]$ (\lbl{cy:uniform-integrality}), and
 $|v_2(u_x(0))-D(h_x)|\le C_{\rm loc}$ (\lbl{cy:central}), where
 $D(h)=v_2(\cL(h))-2w(h)-s(h)$. One applies Lemma~\ref{lem:A} to the
 constant coefficients.
\item \emph{Ring class, rank $1$} (\lbl{rd:missing-vertex},
 \src{ring-limits}{64}). The coordinates are not uniformly integral.
 Instead there is a \emph{clearing factor} $f$ with $f_xu_x=d_x$, with
 $v(f_0(0))$ and the Laurent pole order of $f$ bounded independently of $b$
 (\lbl{rd:family-clearing}). Lemma~\ref{lem:A} is applied to finitely many
 Laurent coefficients of $f$ and to the constant coefficient of $d$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Proof architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

\subsection{Rank zero: the cyclotomic route}
If $s_2(E)=0$, the root number is $+1$ (parity, \lbl{pr:invariance}).
\begin{enumerate}
\item An \emph{even coefficient system} (\lbl{co:even}): a weight-$3/2$
 form whose squarefree coefficients satisfy, within one local squareclass,
 the exact Waldspurger ratio $2v(a(D)/a(D_0))=v(L_*(\sigma D)/L_*(\sigma D_0))$.
\item With the cyclotomic lower bound \lbl{cy:lower-bound}, this defines a
 binary \emph{symbol} $A(D)\in\{0,1\}$: $A(D)=1$ iff $a(D)$ has the minimal
 normalized depth $\delta+w(D)$ (\lbl{co:minimum}). A unit symbol gives
 $L_*\ne0$ and $v(L_*)\le2w+C$.
\item The address lemma \lbl{gr:address} adjoins prime networks so that the
 symbol is $1$ at every nonzero address of a cube based at $h=1$.
\item Cyclotomic transfer \lbl{cy:missing-vertex} gives $L(E,1)\ne0$
 (\lbl{pw:even-base}, \src{pointwise}{306}).
\end{enumerate}
The same construction, with a stationary base, gives the uniform \emph{even
estimate} of \lbl{pw:partitionable-even}, namely $v(L_*(h))-2w(h)-s(h)\le C$ at every
``partitionable'' $h$ of Selmer corank $0$.

\subsection{Rank one: three companions}
If $s_2(E)=1$, the root number is $-1$. There are three distinct auxiliary
discriminants. Keeping them apart is the main bookkeeping point of
\S\S7--9.
\begin{description}
\item[$h_*<0$ (fixed detector partner).] $L(E^{(h_*)},1)\ne0$. For $h>0$,
 the genus-character Heegner sum $P_h$ over $\Q(\sqrt{hh_*})$ has height
 $\sim L'(E^{(h)},1)L(E^{(h_*)},1)$, so it \emph{detects} a simple zero of
 $E^{(h)}$. Its reductions modulo an auxiliary prime~$\ell$, through the supersingular
 points of $X_0(N)$, give the
 \emph{odd coefficients} (\lbl{co:theta-bridge}, \lbl{co:odd}).
\item[$k'(h)<0$ (varying, bounded prime count).] To normalize the odd
 coefficients one needs the lower bound $2j(P_h)\ge2w(h)+s(h)-C$,
 uniform in the prime count of $h$ (\lbl{pw:odd-lower}). For each $h$ of
 analytic rank one, choose $k'$ with $\omega(k')\le A$ and
 $L(E^{(hk')},1)\ne0$ (\lbl{pw:rough-companion}, using the bounded-factor
 nonvanishing \lbl{nv:rough}). Then combine three things: the ring-class
 lower bound over $\Q(\sqrt{k'})$ (\lbl{rd:ring-lower}, uniform in
 $\omega(k')\le A$); the even estimate at $hk'$; and the Gross--Zagier
 height comparison \lbl{pw:height-comparison},
 $2j_h(k)-2j(P_h)=v(L_*(hk))+O(1)$.
\item[$k<0$ (fixed final companion).] It is chosen with
 $L(E^{(k)},1)\ne0$ \emph{before} any cube dimension. Then
 $s_2(E/K)=s_2(E)+s_2(E^{(k)})=1$ for $K=\Q(\sqrt k)$
 (\lbl{pw:quadratic-corank}). Cubes based at $h=1$ have all new primes
 split in $K$ and two simultaneous unit symbols at each $x\ne0$: odd at
 $h_x$ and even at $h_xk$. These give $2j_{h_x}(k)-4w-s(h_x)-s(h_xk)\le C$,
 and the fixed-$K$ ring-class transfer \lbl{rd:missing-vertex} returns
 $\ord_{s=1}L(E,s)L(E^{(k)},s)=1$, hence $a(E)=1$ (\lbl{pw:odd-base}).
\end{description}

\begin{inwords}
$h_*$ defines the detector, $k'(h)$ proves its lower bound, and the fixed $k$
permits the final transfer (\src{introduction}{138--139}).
\end{inwords}

\paragraph{Order of choices.}
The ledger at \src{preliminaries}{127--151} is worth reading first. Fixed
arithmetic constants come first: the curve, the parametrization, the fixed
support and local classes. Varying companions may depend only on a bound $A$
for their prime count. The fixed transfer companion $K$ is chosen before $b$.
Then come $b$, the finite networks, the coefficient precision, and an
arithmetic ``stage'' (a precision $M$ and a reduction prime $\ell$). Prime
sizes may grow with $b$; valuation constants may not.

\section{Classical steps needing $p$ odd, and their replacements}
\label{sec:replace}

Every entry was checked against the source text. ``Sah's lemma'' is our name
for the argument; the paper does not use the name.

\toprule

\midrule
\endhead
$\tau$-eigenspaces $(1\pm\tau)/2$ &

 exact-kernel element $u$, $b((\tau u)^2)=(1+\tau)b(u)$, and

\section{Section-by-section guide}
\label{sec:walk}

Headings refer to the \emph{paper's} sections. For each we give (a) the
main statements, (b) the idea, (c) the key computation, and (d) what a
referee should check.

\subsection*{\S2 Preliminaries \hfill \src{preliminaries}{1--151}}
(a) \lbl{pr:ranks}: $s_2=r+\corank\Sha[2^\infty]$, and analytic rank $\le1$
gives $r=a$ and $\Sha$ finite. \lbl{pr:invariance}: $(-1)^{s_2(A)}=
\varepsilon(A)$ (Monsky \cite{Monsky1996}; Dokchitser--Dokchitser
\cite{DokchitserDokchitser2010}); isogeny invariance; and
$s_2(A/K)=s_2(A)+s_2(A^{(k)})$, proved with $1\pm c$ only after
rationalization.
(b) These fix the corank bookkeeping. (d) Check that the quadratic identity
is used only as a corank identity (the paper says so at
\src{preliminaries}{76}). Read the ledger at lines 127--151.

\subsection*{\S3 Cyclotomic interpolation \hfill \src{cyclotomic}{1--788}}
(a) \lbl{cy:lower-bound}: $v_2(\cL(h))\ge2w(h)+s(h)-C_E$ whenever
$L(E^{(h)},1)\ne0$, uniformly in the number and size of the primes of $h$.
\lbl{cy:missing-vertex}: as in Section~\ref{sec:mv} of this guide, with conclusion
$L(E^{(h_0)},1)\ne0$ and $D(h_0)\le B+C_E$.

(b) \emph{The object.} Work over $R=\Lambda[G]$ with
$\mathbb T=T\otimes R$, twisted by $\chi_{h_0}$, by the universal quadratic
character $\psi:G_\Q\to G$, and by the inverse cyclotomic character. One
complex carries all $2^b$ twists and the cyclotomic variable. After base
change to the local ring $\cO[G]$, whose residue field is $\F_2((t))$, the
positive complex is \emph{free of rank two in degree one}
(\lbl{cy:residual}). Its determinant is therefore a free rank-one module,
and the scalar $u$ defined by $a\wedge\widetilde{2z_S}=u\mathbf e$ is a
single element of $R[1/2]$ whose characters are the twists. The real-place
class $a$ is the extra generator that makes the rank $2$; it is present
because $\Q$ has a real place.

\emph{Why two coefficient rings.} The ring $\cO=\widehat{\Lambda_{(2)}}$ is a
DVR with residue field $\F_2((t))$, so $\cO[G]$ is local and every element
with nonzero image in $\F_2((t))$ is a unit. This is what makes the free
model have rank exactly $2$, independently of $b$ and of the support. But
$\cO$ has no evaluation $t=0$ (\src{cyclotomic}{71--75}): an element of
$\cO$ is, modulo $2^M$, a Laurent series with finite negative part. Central
values must therefore be read in $\Lambda[1/2]$. Lemma \lbl{cy:fourier} is
the bridge: an element that is integral over $\cO$ and characterwise in
$\Lambda[1/2]$ is integral over $\Lambda$, with no loss depending on $|G|$.

(c) Key computations.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Two-sided integrality.} Kato's classes (8.1.2--8.1.3), traced
 through one fixed form lattice and pushed along
 $[\sigma]\mapsto\chi_{h_0}(\sigma)[\sigma|_{\Gamma_n}][\psi(\sigma)]$
 (\lbl{cy:coefficient-map}), give $2^{C_{\rm int}}u\in\cO[G]$ with no
 division by $|G|$. Kato 12.5(3) with Euler corrections gives
 $u_x\in\Lambda[1/2]$ for each character (\lbl{cy:rational-membership}).
 \lbl{cy:fourier} then gives $2^{C_{\rm int}}u\in\Lambda[G]$.
\item \emph{Central formula} (\lbl{cy:central}). The integral Poitou--Tate
 sequence gives $\length H^2=s-i+\sum_q\length H^0(\Q_q,A_h)+O(1)$
 (\lbl{cy:pt-length}). The index $i$ cancels. Unused good primes give
 $v_2\#E^{(h_x)}(\F_q)$, which cancels the imprimitive Euler factors in
 Kato's reciprocity (\lbl{cy:reciprocity}). Active primes give exactly
 $t_q$, summing to $2w(h_x)$.
\item \emph{Bad primes.} The correct local block is $H^1(I_q,V_h)$, not
 $V_h^{I_q}(-1)$ (\src{cyclotomic}{486--498}; the split multiplicative
 example is worth reading).
\end{itemize}

(d) Referee checks: the rational comparison (\lbl{cy:rational-comparison},
derived at \src{cyclotomic}{523--537}), which is \emph{sketched}. The
argument is that two rank-one Iwasawa classes agree at infinitely many
finite-order characters, and then Weierstrass preparation. Check the
uniformity of $C_{\rm int}$ in $S,h_0,G,b$ (\src{cyclotomic}{620--622}), and
the choice of $c,d$ in unsmoothing (Kato 8.1.2 needs $\mathrm{prime}(cd)\cap
S=\emptyset$; taking $c,d$ prime suffices). Also check Ferrero--Washington at
$p=2$ for the real cyclotomic tower in \lbl{cy:residual}.

\subsection*{\S4 Ring-class infrastructure \hfill \src{ring-limits}{1--981}}
(a) Statements of the two outputs, \lbl{rd:ring-lower} and
\lbl{rd:missing-vertex} (lines 42--89). These are followed by the
construction lemmas, packaged in \lbl{rl:limit-evaluation-package}
(line 941).

(b) \emph{What object is being built, and why limits.} We want a perfect
Selmer complex over $\Lambda=\Z_2[[t]]$ for $T\chi_h\Psi$, where $\Psi$ is a
``ring-class Iwasawa variable'' at an auxiliary split prime. Its specialization
$t=0$ should be the Kummer Selmer complex of $E^{(h)}/K$, and its residual
fiber over $\F_2((t))$ should have bounded size. (Our gloss, not a sentence of the paper: ring-class
extensions of $r$-power conductor have finite $2$-part, and the
anticyclotomic $\Z_2$-extension ramifies above $2$, where $\Psi$ is required
to be trivial.) The paper therefore uses,
at precision $i$, a \emph{different} prime $r_i$ with a character $\xi_i$
of order $2^{n_i}\to\infty$ (\lbl{rl:characters}), and coefficients
$A_i=(\Z/2^i)[t]/(t^i)$. The finite complexes for different $i$ form no
inverse system. However, \lbl{rl:finite-model} gives bounded free models with
explicit contractions $(\iota_i,p_i,h_i)$ to the actual cochains, and
$A_i$ is finite at each fixed precision. A nonprincipal ultrafilter therefore
selects entrywise limits of all matrices, including homotopies and duality
data (\lbl{rl:finite-limits}).

Two subtleties drive the length of the section. First, a limit cycle
need not lift to a genuine finite cycle, and one needs \emph{actual} Galois
cocycles in order to evaluate at Frobenius. This is the job of
\lbl{rl:evaluation}: evaluation matrices $e^1_{g,i}$ are kept for
\emph{every} sequence $g=(g_i)\in\prod_iG_K$, with the cocycle identities
\lbl{rl:eval-boundary}--\lbl{rl:eval-cocycle}. Second, ring-class fields of
unbounded degree are never objects of the limit. Descent, Shapiro transfer
and coset sums are done at each finite stage, and only target-side data on
$K$ are retained (``Target-side data'', \src{ring-limits}{219--281}).

The \emph{outer} limit (\lbl{rl:outer-evaluation}) is a compactness device.
If losses at $(2)$ were unbounded along a sequence of instances with
bounded model size, the ultraproduct DVR $\cO_\infty$ (characteristic $0$)
would carry a limiting complex on which the characteristic-$0$ rank
reduction of \S5 runs, and this gives a contradiction.

Schematically (our notation; the top row is the inner limit for one
instance, the bottom row the outer limit over instances $j$):
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.4em,row sep=1.8em]
\bigl\{(C_i,d_i,\iota_i,p_i,h_i,e^1_{g,i},\dots)\bigr\}_i\ \text{over } A_i
 \arrow[r,"\lim_{\mathcal U}"] &
 C\ \text{over }\Lambda \arrow[d,"{\text{instance } j:\ \otimes\cO_j}"]\\
C_\infty\ \text{over }\cO_\infty &
 \{C_j\}_j\ \text{over }\cO_j \arrow[l,"\prod_{\mathcal U}/\mathfrak a"']
\end{tikzcd}
\]
All matrices have bounded size. Every asserted identity (chain map,
homotopy, duality, cocycle relation) is a finite polynomial identity among
their entries, so it passes to the limit. What does \emph{not} pass
automatically is the existence of the finite-level arithmetic data. These
must be constructed simultaneously at every stage, with one fixed ultrafilter
and the same original stages when new derivative primes are added. This is
exactly where the audit's Gap~1 lies.

(c) Key points. \lbl{rl:kummer}: integral Kummer conditions at $2$ keep
$B(F)[2^n]$ in derived degree $0$. \lbl{rl:local-acyclic}: at $r$, inertia
minus one is $t\cdot$unit, and at active split primes Frobenius is $zB$ with
$z=(1+t)^a$, $a$ odd, so it is nonconstant over $\F_2((t))$.
\lbl{rl:kernel}: the central element $z=g^2\tau g^2\tau^{-1}$.
\lbl{rl:chebotarev-evaluation}: derivative primes with Frobenius $\tau u$,
evaluating at $(\tau u)^2$; then $a_\ell\to0$ and $\ell\to-1$.

(d) Referee checks: (i) the ``Target-side data'' paragraph and
\lbl{rl:limit-evaluation-package} (lines 219--281 and 941--981) are written
as checklists pointing to sketched constructions (Audit Gap~1). (ii) In the
Kummer orthogonality of \lbl{rl:kummer} (lines 320--341), the nullhomotopy is
asserted, not exhibited (Gap~2). (iii) Check that one fixed ultrafilter and
the same original stages are used after every new switch
(\src{ring-limits}{206--216}; \src{ring-determinants}{397--404}). (iv) Check
the uniform constant $c_E$ in \lbl{rl:trace-bound}, including the CM case
with $\chi_{\rm cyc}(\gamma)=625$.

\subsection*{\S5 Switches, determinants, transfer \hfill \src{ring-determinants}{1--1769}}
(a) \lbl{rd:derivative}: an integer $L\ne0$, independent of the number of
primes, such that Heegner derivative classes satisfy $f(P_{I\cup\ell})=0$ and
$s(P_{I\cup\ell})=Jf(P_I)$. \lbl{rd:det-switch}: the valuation is
invariant under a switch. \lbl{rd:away-two}: the coordinate lies in
$\Lambda[1/2]$. \lbl{rd:outer-bound}: $v_{(2)}(u)\ge-C(E,A)$.
\lbl{rd:central-formula}:
\[
 v(u_h(0))=2j_h-s(h)-s(hk)-4w(h)+O_{E,A}(1).
\]
\lbl{rd:family-clearing}: a clearing factor with bounds independent of
$b,\#Q$. \lbl{rd:missing-vertex} is proved at line 1701.

(b) The determinant coordinate $u_I$ pairs the Heegner class with its own
dual functional $\lambda_I$, obtained from global duality through local
contractions. Hence its central value is $2j_h$ (point and functional each
contribute $j_h$) minus the torsion lengths. A Kolyvagin switch at an inert
$\ell$ replaces the finite plane by the singular plane and preserves
$v(u)$ exactly. Repeated switches lower the Selmer dimension of a test fiber,
by two at each rank-two switch (a one-sign fiber first needs one preparatory
switch), until a $1\times1$ model makes the coordinate visibly integral. The terminal minor estimate \lbl{rd:minor},
$u_i=\pm\alpha_i\beta_i/\det A_i$, then bounds the loss.

(c) Key computations. Kolyvagin's local formulas at $2$ (lines 300--378):
$f=(a_\ell F_\ell-(\ell+1))\widetilde Q$,
$s=(a_\ell-(\ell+1)F_\ell)\widetilde Q$, and the identity
$(F_\ell-a_\ell)(a_\ell F_\ell-(\ell+1))=a_\ell-(\ell+1)F_\ell$, so
$s=(F_\ell-a_\ell)f$. The half factor needs $v_2(\ell+1)\ge a+1$. In the
determinant switch, the vector $v_0=2^de_1$ is paired with the fractional
$2^{-d}e_2$: unit volume, with no integral complement needed
(lines 553--564). The central trace at $r_i$ is $m_i=a_{r_i}-2\chi_h(r_i)$,
with $v_2(m_i)\le c_E$. The $\Sha$ comparison
\lbl{rd:sha-comparison} gives
$\length\Sha(E^{(h)}/K)[2^\infty]=s(h)+s(hk)+\sum_{p\mid k}t_p+O_E(1)$.

For the family: the universal trace class is built over $A_i[G]$
\emph{before} character evaluation. Unused primes are removed by $\eta$, and
the new local blocks (residual $t$-order at most $4$) are eliminated by Schur
complements with uniform Laurent pole bounds (\lbl{rd:schur}). This gives
$f$ with $v(f_0(0))\le c_*$ and poles $\le L(M)$.

(d) Referee checks: \lbl{rd:assembly} (lines 1542--1604) is the main
\emph{sketched} assembly (Gap~1). Its hypotheses ``single simultaneous
construction'' (lines 767--770) are exactly what the infrastructure must
deliver; the paper's own Remark \lbl{rd:interface-status} stresses this.
Re-derive \lbl{rd:det-switch} and the half-factor step yourself (our audit
did). Check that Howard is used only as a pattern (lines 407--408, 641):
his odd-prime hypotheses are never invoked. The base step uses Gross--Zagier
at $h=1$ via Cai--Shu--Tian (line 1732). The analogous citation at
\src{ring-limits}{33} should also point to Cai--Shu--Tian with $c=|h|$
(audit minor note).

\subsection*{\S6 Auxiliary twists \hfill \src{nonvanishing}{1--425}}

$+1$ case one can also require $\omega(d)\le A_0$ with $A_0$
\emph{absolute}.
(b) A first moment in a fixed progression with an imposed divisor $b$,
$S_{0,b}(X)=cX\prod_{p\mid b}\beta_p+O(b^BX^{1-\eta})$ (\lbl{nv:moment}),
has nonnegative terms by Waldspurger. A lower linear sieve
\cite[Thm.~11.13]{FriedlanderIwaniec2010} then gives almost-prime $d$,
following Hoffstein--Luo \cite{HoffsteinLuo1997}.
(c) Absolute exponents: the Poisson identity \lbl{nv:poisson}; explicit
local Gauss sums $G_\nu(p^r)$; the bound $(C_0C_1)^{\omega(J\nu)}\le(2J|\nu|)^C$.
(d) The Poisson and omitted-factor steps are compressed (Gap~2). Check that

\subsection*{\S7 Coefficient systems \hfill \src{coefficients}{1--1028}}
(a) \lbl{co:even} (even system and exact ratio); \lbl{co:kummer} and
\lbl{co:theta-bridge}: for $\ell$ with $\rho_T(\Fr_\ell)\equiv C\pmod{2^M}$,
$(h_*/\ell)=-1$, $(D_w/\ell)=1$ (hence $\ell\equiv-1\bmod2^M$),
\[
 a_M(D)\equiv c_0\,2^{\omega(N)}\zeta_M(D)\,\lambda_M\bigl(\mathrm{red}_\ell(LP_D)\bigr)\pmod{2^M}.
\]
\lbl{co:odd}: $v(a_M(D))\ge\min\{M,j(P_D)-C\}$, and a visible coefficient
bounds $j(P_D)$. Then \lbl{co:minimum}, \lbl{co:operators}, \lbl{co:traces},
\lbl{co:inertia}, and the \lbl{co:symbol-interface}.

(b) Even side: in one squareclass, Waldspurger's comparison is an
\emph{equality} of squares, so valuations are exact, with no product of local
factors over the new primes. Odd side: a weighted ternary theta series on the
definite quaternion algebra ramified at $\ell,\infty$. Its Schwartz function
carries local weights: a selected-root character at level primes,
$\epsilon_p$ on nilpotent orbits at $p\mid h_*$, and a residue-line character
at $\ell$. Its $|h_*|D$-coefficients count optimal embeddings weighted by the
genus character, which reproduces $\lambda_M$ of the reduced genus sum.
After normalization by the minimum depth $\delta$, coefficients become
\emph{bits}. Divided operators $B_U$ and unary-corrected $H_U$ turn Hecke
traces at fresh primes into tests of single bits (``fresh-prime testing'').

(c) The contraction identity (\lbl{co:contraction}). Changing one oriented
ramification bit at $p\in U$ changes the symbol by $A((U\setminus p)\cup q')$
with $\Fr_{q'}\sim g\phi_p$: two prime labels merge, and the
\emph{whole} rational prime $p$ is omitted. Two simple vertices give zero.
The good-twin rule: inserting two simple primes with equal labels preserves
the symbol when $\#E(\F_q)/2$ is odd.

(d) Referee checks: lattice preservation by $t(u),d(u)$ (via Chebotarev
approximation by $T_q$, $q\langle q\rangle$) and the derivation of
$H_U[q^2]$ (lines 793--849, 1000--1020) are sketched (Gap~2). Also check the
Waldspurger hypotheses (H1), (H2) (lines 93--95), and that the odd
normalization is \emph{conditional} until \lbl{pw:odd-lower} (lines 566--583).

\subsection*{\S8 Arithmetic graphs \hfill \src{graphs}{1--646}}
(a) \lbl{gr:realization}: any prescription of independent residue bits
involving new vertices is realized by fresh primes (ray classes, Hilbert
reciprocity fixes the reverse bits); product clusters can be replaced by
single primes. \lbl{gr:address} (line 199): given terminals with nonzero
terminal polynomials at each $x\ne0$, for one or two symbols, one can adjoin
primary auxiliaries, absent at $0$, so that every symbol equals $1$ at every
$x\ne0$.

(b) Differentiating in an edge bit contracts that edge (the symbol
interface). Trees therefore contract, and cycles differentiate to zero
(\lbl{gr:forest}). A network of auxiliary vertices acts on the terminal
polynomial by a sum of contraction operators. By the all-minors matrix-tree
theorem the constant term is $\det Q$ and the pair-contraction term is
$c_a^{\mathsf t}\adj(Q)c_b$ (\lbl{gr:network}), where $Q$ is the grounded
Laplacian over $\F_2$.

(c) \lbl{gr:programs} builds networks in $b$ groups of three vertices
activated by a basis $\lambda_i$ with $\lambda_i(x_*)=1$. These are present
together only at $x_*$. The form $H(T,B)=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&T\\
T^{\mathsf t}&B\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ uses $T=I+P$ (cyclic shift) for the
``singular'' program, or $T=I+L$ for the pair $\mathcal N^{(\varepsilon)}$.
For two symbols, the top parts are multiplied in the polynomial ring (not
the Boolean ring) and the sum over $\varepsilon$ is shown to be $1$
(lines 559--625).

(d) Our audit brute-forced the combinatorics (400 random graphs, about 200
full runs of \lbl{gr:address}, 0 failures). The referee should focus on the
arithmetic input: that the symbols really satisfy whole-prime omission
and primary isolation (\S7 and \S\S9.5--9.6). Note the closing caveat
(lines 637--646): this section proves no uniformity in $b$.

\subsection*{\S9 Assembly \hfill \src{pointwise}{1--1058}}
(a) \lbl{pw:interfaces}; \lbl{pw:partitionable-even}; \lbl{pw:even-base};
\lbl{pw:rough-companion}; \lbl{pw:height-comparison}; \lbl{pw:odd-lower};
\lbl{pw:split-witness}; \lbl{pw:odd-base}; the proof of \lbl{thm:main}
(line 1029).

(b) Isogeny orientation: use a full-$2$-torsion curve in the isogeny class
if there is one; otherwise $F,F'$ are quadratic and ``good simple'' primes
have $\#E(\F_p)\equiv2\bmod4$ (\lbl{pw:good-simple}). Seeds, reserves and
neutral packets (lines 149--274) are finite label combinatorics. They show
that a long list of terminal primes can be contracted to a minimum seed
plus good twin pairs, so the terminal polynomial is nonzero. One packet is
put on every nonzero linear form of $\F_2^b$. The odd symbol is stabilized
along an ultrafilter of precisions $M$ (lines 499--549); an actual stage is
chosen only after $b$ and the networks.

(c) The full-$2$-torsion case needs an extra argument
(\lbl{pw:split-witness}). After restricting to $K$-split primes the even
minimum might sit only at the empty configuration. The paper shows
$a_p(E^{(k)})\equiv p+1\pmod8$ for split $p$, divides once more by $2$

in $K$ and trivial on $E[8]$. The orientation correction

(d) Check the order of choices in \lbl{pw:odd-base}: $k$ is fixed before

\lbl{pw:split-witness} should state that $k$ is a square at $2N$.

\subsection{A cube for $E=$ 14a1}
Take $E:\ y^2+xy+y=x^3+4x-6$, conductor $14$, $E(\Q)_{\rm tors}\cong\Z/6$, so
$E(\Q)[2]=\Z/2$. Its discriminant is $-21952=-2^6\cdot7^3$, so
$F=\Q(E[2])=\Q(\sqrt{-7})$ and $t_q=2$ iff $(\frac{-7}q)=1$. With PARI
(computation ours) we get $L(E,1)/\Omega^+_E=1/6$, so $v_2(\cL(1))=-1$; this
matches BSD with $c_2c_7=6$ and $\#E(\Q)_{\rm tors}=6$.

The filter of $h_0=1$: $d>0$, $d\equiv1\bmod8$, $(\frac d7)=1$. A cube with
$b=2$ uses $\lambda_{-3}=\lambda_{-19}=x_1$ and $\lambda_5=\lambda_{13}=x_2$:
\[
 h_{00}=1,\quad h_{10}=57=(-3)(-19),\quad h_{01}=65=5\cdot13,\quad
 h_{11}=3705.
\]
All four lie in the filter.

\emph{What to expect (heuristic, assuming BSD; not used anywhere).} For a
rank-$0$ twist, BSD writes $\cL(h)$ as $\#\Sha\cdot\prod_pc_p$ over torsion,
times period comparisons. At an active prime $p\mid h$ the twist has
additive reduction, and the $2$-part of $c_p$ is $2^{t_p}$ (the paper uses
this at \src{ring-determinants}{1170--1178}). Up to bounded local factors at
$2N$, one expects $v_2(\cL(h))=2w(h)+s(h)+O(1)$, i.e.\ $D(h)=O(1)$.
Theorem \lbl{cy:lower-bound} proves the lower half of this without BSD. The table lists $\cL(d)=\sqrt d\,L(E^{(d)},1)/

\subsection{The smallest address network}
The paper's concrete instance (\src{graphs}{435--454}): $b=1$, target $x_*=1$,
two always-active split terminals $a_0,a_1$ with selected bit $z$, and three
primary vertices $1,2,3$ activated by $x$. The program gives the path
\[
 a_0 - 1 - a_1 - 3 - 2,\qquad
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\0&1&1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 c_{a_0}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 c_{a_1}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
We checked by enumeration: (i) $Q$ is the grounded Laplacian over $\F_2$ of
this path; (ii) the forests with exactly one terminal per component are the
two obtained by deleting $a_0\!-\!1$ or $1\!-\!a_1$, so $\det Q=2\equiv0$;
(iii) the only forest joining $a_0,a_1$ with all vertices covered is the
whole path, and $\adj(Q)=e_1e_1^{\mathsf t}$, so
$c_{a_0}^{\mathsf t}\adj(Q)c_{a_1}=1$. \emph{In words:} at $x=1$ the network
acts on the terminal polynomial exactly by $\Delta_z$; at $x=0$ it is absent
and acts by the identity. If the symbol's polynomial is $z$ (a bit that must
be flipped), the network flips it at $x=1$ only.
