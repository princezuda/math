\section{The theorem and why it is hard}

\begin{statement}[\lbl{thm:main}, \src{01:7}]
There is no algorithm which, given $f\in\Z[X_1,\dots,X_n]$, with $n$ part
of the input, decides whether $f$ has a zero in $\Q^n$.
\end{statement}

\paragraph{The classical route and its barrier.}
Over $\Z$ the problem is undecidable by Davis--Putnam--Robinson--Matiyasevich
\cite{DavisPutnamRobinson1961,Matiyasevich1970}.  The obvious transfer to
$\Q$ would be an \emph{existential} (Diophantine) definition of $\Z$ in $\Q$,
or more generally a Diophantine model of $(\Z,+,\cdot)$ in some $\Q^k$.
The known definitions have extra quantifiers.  Robinson defined $\Z$ in
$\Q$ by a first-order formula \cite{Robinson1949}.  Poonen used a formula of
shape $\forall^2\exists^7$ \cite{Poonen2009}.  Koenigsmann gave a universal
definition \cite{Koenigsmann2016}.  None of these is existential.

Mazur's conjecture on the topology of rational points \cite{Mazur1995}
would rule out a Diophantine definition of $\Z$ in $\Q$.  By
Cornelissen--Zahidi \cite[Thm.~3.6]{CornelissenZahidi2000}, it would also
rule out any Diophantine model of $\Z$ in a power $\Q^k$.  So the direct
route is widely expected to be impossible.

\paragraph{How the preprint evades it.}
The preprint proves a \emph{Turing} reduction
$\mathrm{H10}(\Z)\le_T\mathrm{H10}(\Q)$.  For each nonconstant $f$ it
effectively lists finite ``tests''.  Each test is a finite system of
rational-solvability questions, and
\[
 f\ \text{has a zero in }\Z^n
 \iff \text{every test succeeds}
 \qquad(\lbl{intro:finite-test-interface},\ \src{01:176}).
\]
The right side is an infinite conjunction of existential statements over
$\Q$.  No single Diophantine set is shown to equal $\Z$, and no model of
arithmetic is built.  The preprint says so explicitly: the construction
``gives no single existential definition of $\Z$ in $\Q$''
(\src{07:295}).  Our audit agrees that the result does not conflict with
Mazur's conjecture or with Cornelissen--Zahidi.

\paragraph{Lineage.}
Rank-one elliptic curves as carriers of integer indices go back to Poonen
\cite{PoonenRankOne2002,PoonenSubrings}.  The number-field analogue, for
rings of integers, was recently settled unconditionally by Koymans--Pagano
\cite{KoymansPagano2026} and by Alp\"oge--Bhargava--Ho--Shnidman
\cite{AlpogeBhargavaHoShnidman2026}.  Those results concern finitely
generated $\Z$-algebras, a class that does not contain $\Q$.  The new
ingredient here is a \emph{height bound}, which is used to show that a
nonstandard ``solution'' passing every test must in fact be standard.

\section{The proof in one page}\label{sec:onepage}

Fix a nonconstant $f$.  Assume every test succeeds, and suppose for
contradiction that $f$ has no integer zero.

\paragraph{Step 0: the decision procedure.}
Integer zeros are recursively enumerable.  Given an oracle for
$\mathrm{H10}(\Q)$, failed tests are recursively enumerable too.  Run both
searches in parallel.  The arithmetic content is that \emph{some test fails
whenever $f$ has no integer zero} (\src{01:181}, \src{02:876--887}).

\paragraph{Step 1: compactness.}
If all tests succeed, the following exist (Lemma \lbl{red:compactness}):
an elementary extension of the many-sorted structure
$(\Q,\Z,\R,F,\text{valuations},\text{heights},\dots)$; a ring $R$ embedded
in its rational sort $\sQ$; and a root $\mathbf a\in R^n$ with
$f(\mathbf a)=0$.  The ring $R$ satisfies a recursive theory $\mathcal T$,
and every element of $R$ satisfies the pole-parity formula $\Phi$.

\paragraph{Step 2: the index map.}
$\mathcal T$ attaches to each $a\in R$ a point $T_a$ on
$E:y^2=x^3-d^2x$ over $F=\Q(\sqrt2)$, compatibly with addition, with
$T_1=P$.  Here $E(F)$ has rank one and $mE(F)=\Z P$.  Transfer then gives
\[
 \widetilde T_a=\eta(a)P,\qquad \eta:R\to\sZ\ \text{additive, injective},\
 \eta(1)=1.
\]
The map $\eta$ need not be multiplicative, so $f(\eta(\mathbf a))$ need not
vanish.  Put
\[
 \delta=f(\eta(a_1),\dots,\eta(a_n))\in\sZ,\qquad
 B=\max(1,|\eta(a_j)|),\qquad D=\max(1,\deg f).
\]
If $\delta=0$, elementarity gives an ordinary integer zero.  So assume
$\delta\neq0$.

\paragraph{Step 3: slopes and contact primes.}
At each ordinary precision $K$, $\mathcal T$ writes every $A\in R$ as
\[
 A=\frac{u_1+u_2+u_3}{v},\qquad \text{slopes } s_i=(u_i:v)\in\PP^1.
\]
Each of the thirteen \emph{contact values} $v$ and $L_b(u_i,v)$ is required
to be $\pm k\,r$, with $k$ in a fixed finite set and $r$ a ``tested prime''
of $R$.  A prime $q$ of $\sZ$ outside the exceptional set is a
\emph{contact prime} of $s$ if some $L_b(u,v)$ has odd normalized
valuation at $q$.

\paragraph{Step 4: local comparison at every contact prime.}
At such a $q$, some tested $r$ has odd $q$-valuation.  Pole parity and a
coarsening of $v_q$ select one of the two tested branches above $r$.  The
paired elliptic tests there give
\[
 v_q\bigl(\widetilde a-\eta(a)\bigr)\ge K\quad\text{for all }a\in R
 \qquad(\lbl{red:index-congruence}).
\]

\paragraph{Step 5: one error term for all $A$.}
Apply Step~4 to the root $\mathbf a$.  Since $f(\widetilde{\mathbf a})=0$,
we get $v_q(\delta)\ge K$ at every contact prime of every slope.  Then fix
$K\ge cD$, where $c$ is the exponent of the height estimate.  Chain:
\begin{gather*}
 M_K(\widetilde s_i)^K\mid\delta,\qquad
 0<|\delta|\le C_fB^D
 \qquad(\text{\src{02:781}, \src{02:809}})\\
 \Longrightarrow\quad
 h(\widetilde s_i)\le H_KM_K(\widetilde s_i)^c
 \le H_K|\delta|^{c/K}
 \le H_KC_f^{c/K}B^{cD/K}\ll_f B
 \qquad(\text{\src{02:816--826}})\\
 \Longrightarrow\quad
 h(\widetilde A)\le\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^3h(\widetilde s_i)+\log3\ll_f B
 \quad\text{for every }A\in R
 \qquad(\lbl{red:all-ring-height}).
\end{gather*}
The implied constant is ordinary and independent of $A$.

\paragraph{Step 6: canonical height.}
Each $x(T_{a_j})$ equals $(x_0(a_j)+x_1(a_j)\sqrt2)/x_2(a_j)$, built from
three ring elements.  So
$h(x(\eta(a_j)P))\ll_f B$.  Transfer of
$h(x(nP))=2\widehat h(P)n^2+O(1)$, with $\widehat h(P)>0$ \cite{Silverman2009}, then gives
\[
 2\widehat h(P)\,B^2\le CB+C'
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad B\ll_f 1.
\]
Hence every $\eta(a_j)$ is an ordinary integer $n_j$.  Additivity and
injectivity give $a_j=n_j$.  So $f$ has an ordinary integer zero,
contradicting $\delta\ne0$.

\inwords{The tests cannot force $R=\Z$.  They can force every element of
$R$ to agree $q$-adically with its elliptic index, at every prime that the
height estimate needs.  That one fact turns a single nonzero integer
$\delta$ into a uniform height bound on $R$.  Quadratic growth of
canonical height then leaves no room for nonstandard indices.}

The preprint's own one-figure summary of Steps~3--5 is in
\src{figures/reduction-overview.tex} (input at \src{02:790}).

\paragraph{Reading the diagram.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Essential external dependency:} \cite[Thm.~1.1]{PointwiseTwoConverse}
(\src{03:457--462}, \src{03:493}).  Note where it enters.  It proves
\emph{completeness} of $\Phi$ on $\Z$.  Without it, nothing guarantees
that an $f$ with an integer zero passes every test, so a failed test would
no longer certify ``no integer zero'' (\src{02:540--543}).  The preprint's Remark (\src{03:528}) offers
\cite{UnrestrictedTwoConverse} as an alternative.  That is also an OpenAI
preprint.
\item \textbf{Inessential external dependency:}
\cite[Thm.~1.1]{OddRegularFontaineMazurAtTwo}, used for modularity of
$r_2$ (\src{04:902--954}).  The audit records a classical replacement:
$A$ is a Ribet--Pyle building block, hence a factor of a $\mathrm{GL}_2$-type
abelian variety over $\Q$, hence modular by Khare--Wintenberger and Kisin.
Alternatively one can apply Pan's theorem at some $\ell\ge5$ and transfer
the result via \lbl{hei:compatibility}.  (These three works are not in the
preprint's bibliography.)
\item Everything else is either proved in the preprint or is a published
theorem.
\end{itemize}

\section{Section-by-section guide}\label{sec:guide}

\subsection{Preprint \S2: finite rational tests (\src{02-reduction.tex}, 888 lines)}

This is the logical core of the preprint.  Read it in full.

\paragraph{(a) Main statements.}

\emph{Inputs stated here and proved later.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \lbl{ell:rank} (\src{02:27}): $\rank E(F)=1$ (proved in \S5).
\item \lbl{ell:formal} (\src{02:109}): the formal estimates (\S5).
\item \lbl{hei:height} (\src{02:232}): the five-point height estimate (\S4).
\item \lbl{pri:integer-coverage} (\src{02:294}): ordinary contact representations (\S6).
\item \lbl{par:pole-parity} (\src{02:477}): pole parity (\S3).
\end{itemize}

\emph{Lemma \lbl{red:local-comparison}} (\src{02:143}).
Fix ordinary $K\ge1$, a prime $q$ of $\sZ$ with $q\notin S_E$ and $q>K+1$,
and a place $v_*$ of $\sF$ above $q$.  Suppose there are nonzero
$h,j_0\in R$, \emph{independent of $a$}, such that for all $a\in R$ the
following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\widetilde T_h,\widetilde T_{ha}$ reduce to $O$ at $v_*$,
$\ell_K(z(\widetilde T_h))\neq0$, and
\[
 v_*\Bigl(\frac{\ell_K(z(\widetilde T_{ha}))}{\ell_K(z(\widetilde T_h))}-\widetilde a\Bigr)\ge K.
\]
\item $\widetilde T^\sigma_{j_0}$ and $\widetilde T^\sigma_{(h-4)a}$ reduce
to $O$ on $E^\sigma$ at the same $v_*$, and
\[
 v_*\bigl(z(\widetilde T^\sigma_{(h-4)a})/z(\widetilde T^\sigma_{j_0})\bigr)\ge K.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Then every $\widetilde a$ is $q$-integral and
$v_q(\widetilde a-\eta(a))\ge K$.

\emph{Lemma \lbl{red:standard-model}} (\src{02:435}):
$\mathcal T$ is recursive, and $\Z$ with $T_a=aP$ is a model.

\emph{Lemma \lbl{red:compactness}} (\src{02:545}):
if every finite test succeeds, then there are an elementary extension, a
model $R\models\mathcal T$, a root $\mathbf a\in R^n$, and an embedding
$R\hookrightarrow\sQ$ whose image satisfies $\Phi$.

\emph{Lemma \lbl{red:coarsening}} (\src{02:643}):
let $q\notin S_K$ be an ambient prime counted by some
$M_K(\widetilde s_i)$, and $r$ a tested element with $v_q(\widetilde r)$
odd, as in \lbl{red:odd-r}.  Then some branch $\mathfrak p\in\{\mathfrak p_\pm\}$
and some place $v_*$ above $q$ satisfy
\[
 D'\in r^l(R_F)_{\mathfrak p}\ \Longrightarrow\ v_*(\widetilde D')\ge l
\]
for all ordinary $l\ge1$.  Moreover, tested-formal points reduce to $O$ at
$v_*$.

\emph{Proposition \lbl{red:index-congruence}} (\src{02:722}):
for every $q$ counted by $M_K(\widetilde s_i)$ and every $a\in R$, the
element $\widetilde a$ is $q$-integral and $v_q(\widetilde a-\eta(a))\ge K$.

\emph{Proposition \lbl{red:finite-test-success}} (\src{02:755}):
if all tests for $f$ are solvable, then $f$ has a zero in $\Z^n$.

\paragraph{(b) Idea.}
The theory $\mathcal T$ says, in ring language, everything the integers
satisfy that the proof needs:
\begin{itemize}
\item the point axioms;
\item for each $K$, a representation axiom: three slopes whose thirteen
contact values are multiplier times tested prime;
\item at each tested prime, two residue branches carrying the paired
elliptic tests.
\end{itemize}
The tests are the finite fragments of the Skolemized $\mathcal T+f(\mathbf a)=0$
evaluated on ground terms.  They are answered by rational-solvability queries,
with $\Phi$ imposed on every ground term (constraints (a)--(d),
\src{02:518--524}).  Compactness \cite{Marker2002} turns ``all tests succeed'' into a model
$R$ inside $\sQ$.  Two connections then do the work.  An ambient contact
prime $q$ is linked to a tested branch by an odd valuation and pole parity.
That branch is linked to the index map by the local comparison.

\paragraph{(c) Key tricks.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The ``$h-4$'' trick} (proof of \lbl{red:local-comparison},
\src{02:178--218}).  Test (ii) uses only additivity of $\eta$.  It gives
$v_q(\eta((h-4)a))\ge K$, hence
\[
 V_a:=\eta(ha)\equiv4\eta(a),\qquad V_1\equiv4\pmod{q^K}.
\]
So $V_a/V_1$ is $q$-integral and congruent to $\eta(a)$, because $q\ne2$.
Integrality is exactly the hypothesis that the truncated-log estimate
(\lbl{ell:truncation}) needs.  Test (i) then identifies $\widetilde a$ with
$V_a/V_1$ to precision $K$.  The same $h$ must serve both $a$ and $1$; this
is why $h,j_0$ are chosen independently of $a$ (\src{02:220}).
\item \emph{Odd contact forces an odd tested value} (\src{02:623--632}).
Fix $q\notin S_K$, and let $t$ be the smaller of $v_q(\widetilde u_i)$ and
$v_q(\widetilde v)$.  The minimum of the five contact valuations equals
$t$, because some pair of forms has unit determinant.  If some contact
depth is odd, the five valuations do not all have the same parity, so
one is odd.  Its factor $k$ is a $q$-unit, so $v_q(\widetilde r)$ is odd.
\item \emph{Coarsening} (proof of \lbl{red:coarsening}, \src{02:656--717}).
$R$ may have poles at $q$.  Let $J\subset\sZ$ be the convex subgroup
generated by the negative values of $v_q$ on $\widetilde R\setminus\{0\}$; this is an
external construction.  Pole parity, applied to $b$ and $ab$, shows that
every ring valuation lying in $J$ is even.  So the odd $v_q(\widetilde r)$
lies outside $J$, and is positive.  The coarsened valuation
$w_*:{}^*F\to\sZ/J$ is $\ge0$ on $R_F$.  Its center is a prime of $R_F$
containing $r$.  Since $R_F/rR_F\simeq(R/rR)^2$, the center is exactly
$\mathfrak p_+$ or $\mathfrak p_-$.
\item \emph{One $\delta$ for all $A$} (\src{02:792--840}).  The internal
universal statement ``every prime counted by $M_K(\widetilde s_i)$ divides
$\delta$ to order $\ge K$'' is established, and then the transferred
finite-product fact gives $M_K(\widetilde s_i)^K\mid\delta$.  The exponent
$c$ must be fixed, because $K\ge cD$ is chosen once (\src{02:264--266}).
\end{enumerate}

\inwords{The ring $R$ may be wildly nonstandard.  Yet at every prime that
matters for heights, it is locally indistinguishable from its own elliptic
indices.}

\checkhead

\subsection{Preprint \S3: pole parity (\src{03-parity.tex}, 718 lines)}

\paragraph{(a) Main statements.}
\emph{Proposition \lbl{par:pole-parity}} (\src{02:477}).  There are a
positive existential ring formula $\Phi(b)$ with rational coefficients and
a finite set $S_1$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item $\Q\models\Phi(b)$ for every $b\in\Z$;
\item if $b\in\Q^\times$ satisfies $\Phi$ and $q\notin S_1$, then
$v_q(b)<0$ implies $v_q(b)\in2\Z$.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{The formula} (\src{03:50--61}).  $\Phi(b)$ holds if $b=0$, or if for
some multipliers $m\in\mathcal M$, $m_d\in\mathcal D_m$ and some
$t,e\in\Q$ we have
\[
 d=m_db,\quad b'=mb,\quad U=b\,\frac{1-dt^2}{1+dt^2},\quad H'=b'(b'-U^2),
 \quad 1+dt^2\ne0,\ eH'\ne0,
\]
and both $E_e$ and $E_{eH'}$ have a rational point with
$x\in b'\Q^{\times2}$.  Here $E_l:y^2=x(x-l)(x+3l)$.

\emph{Supporting lemmas.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \lbl{par:soundness} (\src{03:71}): property (2), for any lists.
\item \lbl{par:cross-squares} (\src{03:170}): mutual local squares of
$\beta,\theta$.
\item \lbl{par:descent} (\src{03:300}): under matrix conditions,
$\dim_{\F_2}\Sel_2(E_l/\Q)=3$, and $(\beta,1)$ is a Selmer class outside the two-torsion classes.
\item \lbl{par:global-point} (\src{03:464}): that class comes from a
rational point.
\item \lbl{par:simultaneous-matrices} (\src{03:543}): one $n$ works for both
$l=n$ and $l=n\theta$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{(b) Idea.}
\emph{Soundness.}  If $v_q(b)=k<0$ is odd, then $v_q(U)=k$ and
$v_q(H')=3k$.  So $e$ and $eH'$ have opposite valuation parities.  On the
twist whose parameter has even valuation, rescale to a unit parameter.
There every nonzero $x$ has even valuation, which contradicts
$x\in b'\Q_q^{\times2}$.

\emph{Completeness.}  For an integer $b$, choose the multipliers and $t$ by
weak approximation, so that $b'$ and $H'$ are local squares at each other's
odd-support primes.  Then build a squarefree $n$ for which both $E_n$ and
$E_{n\theta}$ have $2$-Selmer rank exactly one beyond torsion, with $(\beta,1)$
as the extra class.  The $2$-converse and Cassels--Tate then promote the
Selmer class to a point.

\paragraph{(c) Key computations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Selmer equations.}  In normalized coordinates
$(3^\delta\prod q_i^{u_i},\prod q_i^{v_i})$ (\src{03:384}), the local
conditions read
\[
 Ku+D_\lambda v=\delta\lambda,\qquad Nv=0,\qquad (\lambda+s)^{\mathsf t}v=0,
\]
with $K+K^{\mathsf t}=ss^{\mathsf t}+D_s$ from quadratic reciprocity
(\src{03:282}).  The matrices follow Monsky's viewpoint
\cite[Appendix]{HeathBrown1994}.  The target $\ker K=\langle\mathbf1,\mathbf1_B\rangle$ and
$\lambda\notin\operatorname{im}K$ force $v=0$, $\delta=0$,
$u\in\ker K$.  Hence there are at most $8$ classes, and
$\tau_1,\tau_2,(\beta,1)$ attain the bound.
\item \emph{The converse step} (\src{03:470--514}).  By
$r+\dim\Sha[2]=1$, the $2^\infty$-Selmer corank is $\le1$.  The pointwise
$2$-converse (hypotheses $C(\Q)[2]\ne0$ and corank $\in\{0,1\}$) gives
$\Sha$ finite.  Cassels--Tate on the finite $2$-primary part then makes
$\dim\Sha[2]$ even, so $\Sha[2]=0$ and $r=1$.
\item \emph{Simultaneous matrices} (\src{03:585--704}).  The two kernel
targets reduce to $K\mathbf1_B=0$ plus nonsingularity of the deleted
matrix of $K^\#=K+\lambda a^{\mathsf t}$.  The $B$--$I$ block is built from
a basis of the even-sum hyperplane.  Adding $J$ toggles diagonal entries
independently, and a nonzero multilinear polynomial over $\F_2$ has a
non-root.
\end{itemize}

\inwords{Odd poles are detected by a sign-type invariant of two quadratic
twists.  Integers pass because one can always choose a twist pair whose
Mordell--Weil groups contain the needed square class.  ``Always'' relies on
a converse theorem that rules out a Selmer class coming from an infinite
$\Sha$.}

\checkhead
\begin{itemize}
\item Positivity of $\Phi$: the disjunction over multipliers is finite, and
$\neq$ is encoded as $az=1$ (\src{03:63--69}).
\item The valuation case analysis in \lbl{par:soundness}, including $t=0$
and $q\ne3$ (\src{03:76--103}).  The audit tested soundness on 18k random
$q$-adic cases.
\item The local Kummer images at $q\nmid 6l$, at $q\mid l$, at $3$, at $2$
(necessity only), and at $\infty$ (\src{03:328--390}).
\item Exact hypotheses of \cite[Thm.~1.1]{PointwiseTwoConverse} as quoted
(\src{03:457--462}): full $2^\infty$-Selmer corank, not just $\Sel_2$.
The family $E_l$ is non-CM, with additive reduction at every $q\mid l$.
\item Cassels--Tate alternation for the principal polarization
(\src{03:496--514}).  The locator \cite[Cor.~9]{PoonenStoll1999} is
unverified; it is not load-bearing, since Cassels \cite{Cassels1962}
suffices.
\item The Dirichlet realization of prescribed Legendre symbols with
reciprocity-consistent reverses (\src{03:692--703}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Preprint \S4: the five-point height estimate (\src{04-height.tex}, 1326 lines)}

\paragraph{(a) Main statement.}
\emph{Theorem \lbl{hei:height}} (\src{02:232}).  There are five distinct
points $\Cpts\subset\PP^1(\Q)$, including $\infty$, a finite set $S$, and
constants $H,c>0$ with the following property.  For each
$s=(u:v)\in\PP^1(\Q)\setminus\Cpts$,
\[
 h(s)\le H\,M_S(s)^c,
\]
where $M_S(s)$ is the product of the primes $q\notin S$ at which some
$v_q(L_b(u,v))-\min\{v_q(u),v_q(v)\}$ is odd.
\emph{Corollary \lbl{hei:enlarge-exceptions}} (\src{02:270}): enlarging
$S$ changes $H$ but not $c$.

\inwords{If a rational point of $\PP^1$ meets the five points to odd order
only at a few primes, its height is polynomially bounded in the product of
those primes.  In terms of the naive height $e^{h(s)}$, this is an
exponential-in-a-power bound; the audit calls it ``exponential-abc
strength, not abc strength''.  The proof uses a finiteness statement that
need not be effective (\src{04:680}).}

\emph{Intermediate results.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \lbl{hei:fiber-field} (\src{04:303}): the fiber field $K_0$ has
bounded degree, is unramified outside $S\cup\{q\mid M\}$, and fibers have
good reduction outside $S$.
\item \lbl{hei:base-height} (\src{04:364}):
$h(\pi(y))\le C(1+\max\{0,h_{\mathrm F}(\mathcal A_y)\})$.
\item \lbl{hei:hom-field} (\src{04:584}): a Galois field $L$ of bounded
degree, unramified outside $T$, containing all homomorphisms between
conjugates.
\item \lbl{hei:sign-splitting} (\src{04:766}): a character $\chi$ of order
$\le CM$ and a cochain $\beta$ with $\beta^2=\chi$ and $\partial\beta=\epsilon$.
\item \lbl{hei:compatibility} (\src{04:986}).
\item \lbl{hei:level-bound} (\src{04:1043}): $N\le CM^a$, with $a=2$ allowed.
\item \lbl{hei:modular-factor} (\src{04:1170}): $J_1(N)\sim_{L'}A\times D_0$.
\item Final bound (\lbl{hei:faltings-final}, \src{04:1293}):
$h_{\mathrm F}(A)\le CM^C$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{(b) Idea.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Take the Shimura curve $X$ of the indefinite quaternion algebra of
discriminant $210$.  It has genus $5$.  Its full Atkin--Lehner quotient
$X^*$ is $\PP^1$ over $\Q$, with exactly five branch values, all rational;
these form $\Cpts$.
\item At fine level $Y\to X$ there is a principally polarized QM abelian
surface family $\mathcal A\to Y$.  Fibers over the same point of $X^*$ are
isogenous.
\item For rational $s$, the local equation $t=z^2$ shows that only odd
contact ramifies the fiber field (\lbl{hei:fiber-field}).
\item A theta-height comparison (\lbl{hei:base-height}) shows that
$h(s)$ is controlled by the Faltings height of a fiber $A$.
\item If the centralizer of $B$ in $\operatorname{End}^0(A)$ is an
imaginary quadratic field, then $A$ is isogenous to $E^2$ with $E$ CM, and
its height is uniformly bounded.  Otherwise $\operatorname{End}^0=B$, and $A$ is isogenous to all its Galois
conjugates.  The descent obstruction is a $\pm1$ cocycle, split by a
cochain $\beta$ with controlled order and inertia.  This yields an odd
two-dimensional $2$-adic representation $r_2$, de Rham with Hodge--Tate
exponents $0,1$.
\item Modularity gives a weight-$2$ newform of level $N\ll M^2$, and $A$
is a factor of $J_1(N)$ over a field of degree $\ll M$.
\item Javanpeykar bounds $h_{\mathrm F}(J_1(N))\ll N^{12}$.  The
Gaudron--R\'emond isogeny theorem, with its explicit dependence on the
dimension, transfers this to $h_{\mathrm F}(A)\ll M^C$.
\end{enumerate}
The final step follows von K\"anel's modular height strategy
\cite{vonKaenel2021}.  The main external tools are Pazuki's theta height
\cite{Pazuki2012}, Javanpeykar's bound for Belyi curves
\cite{Javanpeykar2014}, the Gaudron--R\'emond isogeny theorem
\cite{GaudronRemond2014}, Faltings' theorems \cite{Faltings1983}, and
Carayol's local--global compatibility \cite{Carayol1986}.  The descent
step follows the theory of building blocks \cite{Ribet1992,Pyle1995}.

\paragraph{(c) Key tricks.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Parity from ramification index two} (\src{04:312--346}).  After
an unramified extension the local equation is $t=z^2$.  Even contact
gives unramified lifts.
\item \emph{Normalize degrees, not isogenies} (\src{04:729--756}).  With
$a_g=(\deg\mu_g)^{-1/4}$, the discrepancy is a pure sign, so no bound on
$\deg\mu_g$ is needed.
\item \emph{Cheap splitting} (\src{04:781--851}).  Choose an odd character
modulo each odd prime of $T$ where the Brauer class is nonzero.  The order
is then $\le 2\prod_{q\in T,\,q\ne2}(q-1)\le CM$.  A quadratic twist removes
inertia at primes outside $T$.
\item \emph{Level bound} (\src{04:1062--1146}).  At large $q\in T$ the
representation is tame, so the exponent is $\le2$.  At the finitely many
small primes, a uniform upper-ramification cut (from bounded-degree local
fields and the semistable reduction criterion) gives a uniform Swan bound.
At $q=2$ the conductor is read from $r_3$, via \lbl{hei:compatibility}.
\end{itemize}

\checkhead
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Geometry} (\src{04:85--172}).  No elliptic points (because
$\Q(i)$ and $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$ do not embed in $B$).  Area $8$ gives $g=5$.
Then Riemann--Hurwitz, and five distinct labels $30,42,70,105,210$ (see
\S\ref{toy:rh}).  Cross-check against Long--Maclachlan--Reid (signature
$(0;2^5;0)$) and Nualart Riera \cite{LongMaclachlanReid2006,Nualart2015}.
\item The symplectic embedding and the choice of level (\src{04:199--247}).
\item The theta-height argument and its uniformity in the field of
definition (\src{04:372--549}).  This is the most technical analytic step.
\item The CM case: non-effective but uniform (\src{04:658--680}).
\item The hypotheses of the modularity input (\src{04:902--954}): odd,
irreducible, de Rham with distinct weights, finitely ramified.  If you
prefer, check the classical replacement instead (see \S\ref{sec:diagram}).
\item The conductor exponent at small primes.  The citation of the
semistability criterion (\src{04:1115}) is better given as SGA7~IX~4.7,
per the audit.
\item The arithmetic $N^{12}$ (from $\deg_B\le CN^2$ and $g\le CN^2$), and
the bound $\log\deg\phi\le CM^C$ from \lbl{hei:gr} (\src{04:1207--1295}).
\item The audit found no error here, and no cheap counterexample family.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Preprint \S5: the rank-one curve (\src{05-elliptic.tex}, 189 lines)}

\paragraph{(a) Statements.}
\emph{\lbl{ell:rank}}: $\rank E(F)=1$; fix $m,P$ with $mE(F)=\Z P$.

\emph{\lbl{ell:formal}}: there is a finite set $S_E$ with the following
property.  Let $p\notin S_E$, let $v\mid p$ with $v(p)=1$, and let
$nP,n'P$ reduce to $O$ with $n'\ne0$.  Then
\[
 v\Bigl(\frac{z(nP)}{z(n'P)}\Bigr)=v_p(n/n').
\]
If moreover $p>K+1$ and $v_p(n/n')\ge0$, then $\ell_K(z(n'P))\ne0$ and
\[
 v\Bigl(\frac{\ell_K(z(nP))}{\ell_K(z(n'P))}-\frac n{n'}\Bigr)\ge K.
\]
The same holds for $E^\sigma,P^\sigma$.

\paragraph{(b) Idea.}
The rank comes from a $2$-isogeny descent over $\Q(\sqrt2)$, which is
possible because $\Z[\sqrt2]$ is a PID and totally positive units are
squares.  One finds $|\operatorname{im}\alpha|=8$ and
$|\operatorname{im}\alpha'|=1$, hence $4\cdot2^{\rank}=8$.  The formal
statements come from the logarithm being valuation-preserving on the
formal kernel when $p>2$.

\paragraph{(c) Key computation.}
For $t>K$ and $p>K+1$ one has $t-1-v_p(t)\ge K$ (\lbl{ell:tail-exponent}).
Hence $v(\ell(Z)-\ell_K(Z))\ge v(Z)+K$ whenever $v(Z)\ge1$
(\lbl{ell:tail}), and the truncation error is negligible.

\checkhead
The explicit point $Q$ and the sign argument (\src{05:67--75}).  The
computation at $\mathfrak d$ using $-4=3$, a nonsquare in $\F_7$
(\src{05:84--91}).  The tail inequality, including the edge case $p=K+2$.
The audit verified \S5 by exact computation, including a $400$-digit
$r$-adic test at $r=17,23$.

\subsection{Preprint \S6: prime patterns (\src{06-primes.tex}, 705 lines)}

\paragraph{(a) Statements.}
\emph{Lemma \lbl{pri:pattern}} (\src{06:21}).  There is a finite
$\Kmult\subset\Z_{>0}$ with the following property.  For every $A\in\Z$ and
every real $B>0$ there are integers $u_1,u_2,v$, with $v\ne0$ and
$u_3=Av-u_1-u_2$, such that each of the thirteen numbers $v$ and
$L_b(u_j,v)$ ($b\ne\infty$) equals $\pm kr$ with $k\in\Kmult$ and $r>B$
prime.  Each such $r$ splits in $F$, is good for $E$ and $E^\sigma$, and
at either place above $r$
\[
 \gcd(d_1,d_2)\mid4,\qquad r\nmid d_1d_2,
\]
where $d_1,d_2=\#\widetilde E(\F_r),\#\widetilde E^\sigma(\F_r)$.

\emph{Corollary \lbl{pri:integer-tests}} (\src{06:53}).  At each place
above such an $r$ (large enough for precision $K$), take $j_0=d_2$ and
\[
 h\equiv0\pmod{d_1},\qquad h\equiv4\pmod{d_2r^K}.
\]
Then conditions (i) and (ii) of \lbl{red:local-comparison} hold in $\Z$
for all $a$.  Combining the two results gives
\emph{Corollary \lbl{pri:integer-coverage}}.

\paragraph{(b) Idea.}
Take $r\equiv1\pmod8$ and $r\equiv3\pmod7$.  Then $r$ splits in $F$, and
$7=dd^\sigma$ is a nonsquare mod $r$.  So $E$ and $E^\sigma$ reduce to
opposite twists of $y^2=x^3-x$, with orders $r+1\pm2a$ where $a^2+b^2=r$.
The $2$-part of the gcd is exactly $4$.  An odd common factor $l$ needs
$l\mid a$ and $b^2\equiv-1\pmod l$.  Small such $l$ are excluded by
congruences.  Large $l$ are removed by an upper sieve.  The lower bound
for thirteen simultaneous prime values of linear forms in $(u_1,u_2,v)$
comes from Green--Tao--Ziegler \cite{GreenTao2010,GreenTao2012,GreenTaoZiegler2012}.

\paragraph{(c) Key tricks.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Order of choices} (\src{06:121--125}): first $\Kmult$, then
$A$, a box and a congruence class, then the cutoff $L$, and finally the
scale $X$.  $\Kmult$ must be independent of $A$, because it appears in the
single axiom scheme of $\mathcal T$.
\item \emph{Lower versus upper bounds.}  GTZ gives
$N_{A,L}(X)\ge c_A(\log L)^{-C_0}X^3/(\log X)^{13}$ (\lbl{pri:lower}).  The
patterns with a bad $l>L$ number at most $C_AX^3/(L(\log X)^{13})+o(\cdot)$
(\lbl{pri:total-loss}).  Choose $L$ large.  There is no parity barrier,
because the sieve is used only for upper bounds.
\item \emph{(ours)} Why three slopes rather than one?  If $A=u/v$, then
$L_b(Av,v)=v\,L_b(A,1)$ is a multiple of $v$.  Such values could not all
be $\pm k\cdot(\text{large prime})$ alongside $v$ itself.  Three summands
give three free variables, and thirteen forms with pairwise
non-proportional linear parts (\src{06:186--205}), which is the
finite-complexity setting of GTZ.
\end{itemize}

\checkhead
\begin{itemize}
\item Pairwise independence of the thirteen forms modulo $p$ for $p$ above
a threshold independent of $A$ (\src{06:198--205}).
\item The catalogue construction of $\Kmult$ (\src{06:223--250}).
\item The local factors $\beta_p$ (\src{06:392--406}) and the negligibility
of prime powers.
\item Uniformity of the two upper sieves in $L$, $r$ and $l$
(\src{06:550--668}).
\item The cited form of GTZ (\src{06:355--378}).  The audit suggests adding
Manners or Leng--Sah--Sawhney as a backstop to the blog-hosted 2024
erratum \cite{GreenTaoZiegler2024Erratum}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Worked toy illustrations}\label{sec:toys}

\subsection{Why the point axioms exclude $\Z[1/2]$, but not nonstandard denominators \emph{(ours)}}
Take $R=\Z[1/2]\subset\Q$ in the standard structure, embedded by the
identity, as in the setting of \src{02:63--78}.  Then
$F_R=\Q(\sqrt2)=F$.  The axioms demand $T_{1/2}\in mE(F)=\Z P$, say
$T_{1/2}=nP$.  Additivity gives $P=T_1=2T_{1/2}=2nP$.  Since $P$ has
infinite order, $2n=1$, which is impossible.

The same argument works for any standard rational $1/k$ with $k>1$, since
$k\,\eta(1/k)=\eta(1)=1$ has no solution in $\sZ$.  So a model of the
point axioms contains no standard non-integral rationals.

The argument says nothing about an element such as $1/N$ with $N\in\sZ$
nonstandard.  Then $N$ need not lie in $R$, and additivity only constrains
$\eta(k/N)=k\,\eta(1/N)$ for standard $k$.  This is why the preprint needs
the rest of its machinery: local comparisons, height bounds, and canonical
height growth.  The condition $T_a\in mE(F_R)$, rather than
$T_a\in E(F_R)$, is what makes $\eta$ land in $\sZ$ (\src{02:75--78}).

\subsection{The rank-one curve over $\Q(\sqrt2)$}
The data are $d=75-53\sqrt2$, with $N(d)=75^2-2\cdot53^2=7$, and $d>0$ at
both real embeddings.  The preprint gives (\src{05:67--70})
\[
 w=-5+4\sqrt2=d(1+\sqrt2)^3,\qquad w^3-w=-8d,\qquad
 Q=(-dw,\ 2\sqrt2\,d^2)\in E(F).
\]
To check this: $(1+\sqrt2)^3=7+5\sqrt2$, and $(75-53\sqrt2)(7+5\sqrt2)=-5+4\sqrt2$.
Also $w^3=-605+428\sqrt2$, so $w^3-w=-600+424\sqrt2=-8d$.  Finally
$x^3-d^2x=-d^3(w^3-w)=8d^4=y^2$.

The class of $-dw$ has signs $(-,+)$ at the two real embeddings.  The
torsion classes $1,-1,d,-d$ have equal signs at both.  So
$|\operatorname{im}\alpha|=8$.  Together with $\operatorname{im}\alpha'=1$
and \lbl{ell:isogeny-index}, this gives $8=4\cdot2^{\rank}$, so the rank
is $1$.

\subsection{Integer witnesses at a small split prime \emph{(ours, computed)}}
Take $r=17$.  Then $17\equiv1\pmod8$ and $17\equiv3\pmod7$, as in
\lbl{pri:prime-residues}.  The square roots of $2$ mod $17$ are $6$ and
$11$.  At the place $\sqrt2\mapsto6$ we have $d\mapsto12$ and
$d^\sigma\mapsto2$, with $12\cdot2\equiv7$.  A direct point count gives
\[
 d_1=\#\widetilde E(\F_{17})=20,\qquad d_2=\#\widetilde E^\sigma(\F_{17})=16.
\]
This agrees with $r+1\pm2a$ for $17=1^2+4^2$, $a=\pm1$.  Here
$\gcd(20,16)=4$ and $17\nmid d_1d_2$.

For $K=1$, Corollary \lbl{pri:integer-tests} asks for
$h\equiv0\pmod{20}$ and $h\equiv4\pmod{16\cdot17}$.  Take $h=820$ and
$j_0=16$.  Then $hP$ and $haP$ lie in the formal kernel of $E$, and
$(h-4)a=816a=3\cdot16\cdot17\cdot a$.  So
$v_{17}\bigl((h-4)a/d_2\bigr)\ge1=K$, as test~(ii) requires.

At the other place the roles of the two curves swap: $d_1=16$, $d_2=20$,
$h=1024$, $j_0=20$.  The lemma itself uses only primes above a large
threshold; $r=17$ serves only as illustration.

\subsection{The genus-$5$ cover and its five branch values}\label{toy:rh}
The Eichler area formula gives
$\frac16\prod_{p\mid210}(p-1)=\frac16\cdot1\cdot2\cdot4\cdot6=8=2g-2$,
so $g(X)=5$ (\src{04:93--97}).

$\mathcal W\simeq(\Z/2)^4$ has order $16$, and every nontrivial stabilizer
has order $2$.  Riemann--Hurwitz reads
\[
 8=16\bigl(2g(X^*)-2\bigr)+r\cdot8
 \iff 2g(X^*)-2+\tfrac r2=\tfrac12,
\]
so $r=5-4g(X^*)$.

Fixed points of $w_m$ for $m=30,42,70,105,210$ exist because
$\Q(\sqrt{-m})$ embeds in $B$.  For example, for $m=30$ the only prime of
$210$ not dividing $m$ is $7$, and $-30\equiv5$ is a nonsquare mod $7$
(\src{04:139--146}).  So $r\ge5$, which forces $g(X^*)=0$ and $r=5$.  Each
branch value is rational because its label is Galois-stable.

Note that the five points $\Cpts$ are not written down explicitly in the
preprint.  Only $\infty$ is normalized.

\subsection{Pole parity at $b=1/q$}
Take $q\notin S_1$ and $b=1/q$.  For any witnesses, $v_q(d)=v_q(b')=-1$
and $v_q(U)=-1$, so $v_q(H')=-3$.  One of $e$ and $eH'$, call it $l$, has
even valuation $2a$.  On $Y^2=X(X-L)(X+3L)$ with $L=q^{-2a}l$ a unit, every
nonzero $X$ has even valuation.  But the required $x$ lies in
$b'\Q^{\times2}$, which has odd valuation.  So $\Phi(1/q)$ fails.

The argument gives no obstruction for $b=1/q^2$, and
\lbl{par:pole-parity}(2) does not exclude even poles (\src{02:488}).

\subsection{What the height estimate says when $M=1$ \emph{(ours)}}
If every contact order of $s$ outside $S$ is even, then $M_S(s)=1$ and
$h(s)\le H$.  So only finitely many such $s$ exist.  In the reduction,
$M_K(\widetilde s)^K\mid\delta$ means that the contact radical is at most
$|\delta|^{1/K}$, a ``$K$-th root'' of a single fixed error term.  This
is why the exponent $c$ must be independent of $K$ (\src{02:259--266}).

\paragraph{What is conditional.}
The theorem depends on \cite[Thm.~1.1]{PointwiseTwoConverse}.  Known
$2$-converse results cover CM families or special Heegner families.  The
family here is non-CM, with additive reduction at every prime dividing
$l$.  The alternative \cite{UnrestrictedTwoConverse} is also an OpenAI
preprint.  The audit of the pointwise $2$-converse found no error, with
sketched infrastructure gaps.

The Fontaine--Mazur input \cite{OddRegularFontaineMazurAtTwo} is cited
but not needed.  The audit recommends citing the classical route, noting
that $\ell=2$ is the hardest choice since $B$ is ramified at $2$.  The
Fontaine--Mazur preprint itself needs expert review of three points:
characteristic-$2$ patching, marked-line connectedness, and classicality
transport (which relies on another unrefereed preprint).
